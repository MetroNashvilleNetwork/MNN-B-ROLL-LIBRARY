### Editing Rules to Emulate

1. **OPEN on a close detail shot (3–5s)** — branded item, prop, or award object. Not a person. Not a wide. A single macro that holds long enough to create intrigue.
2. **Shot 2 must also be a detail/close** (~3s) — second branded detail or event artifact. Two close-ups back-to-back before any face appears.
3. **Shot 3 introduces the first human moment** (~1.3s) — candid interaction, NOT a staged pose.
4. **Shot 4 is the establishing wide** (~0.9s) — the audience/room reveal. Pay it off fast.
5. **Shots 5–10: context montage** — average 1.5s per shot, mix of medium and wide, alternating speakers and crowd. Include at least one graphic text overlay layer if available.
6. **Award sequence pattern (repeating unit):** wide_crowd (0.5–1s) → award_moment_angle_1 (0.5–1s) → award_moment_angle_2 (0.25–0.5s) → reaction_or_crowd (0.5s). Each repeat takes ~2–3s total.
7. **Shot lengths compress toward the end** — by the final third, most shots are under 0.6s. The edit accelerates.
8. **Final shot is a LONG HOLD wide group** (2.5–3s minimum) — everyone together, static, let it breathe.
9. **All cuts are hard cuts** — no dissolves, no effects.
10. **Never slow-mo by default** — if raw footage is 60fps, play at 24fps equivalent only for deliberate emphasis; the reference style plays everything at real speed.
11. **Framing rule:** strong preference for medium and close framings. The only wide shots are establishing shots and group finales. Most beauty is in the 2/3-shot and close-up territory.
12. **Camera motion:** locked/static camera is preferred for presentational moments. Handheld is reserved for candid/reaction shots only. The contrast between locked and handheld signals "this matters vs this is real."
